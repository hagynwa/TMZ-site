This is a design mockup, not a live site and not an official Torah MiTzion page. Every community, date, photograph count, and person's name on these boards is invented placeholder data used to show the design working. None of it is a record of any real community or person.

30 Years
1996–2026

כִּי מִצִּיּוֹן תֵּצֵא תוֹרָה

Thirty years of Torah carried out from Zion —
and every face that carried it.

A proposed home for the photographs gathered from Torah MiTzion communities across thirty years: a schematic world map re‑centred on Jerusalem, a timeline you fly through year by year, and a roster for every community‑year — Rosh Kollel, his household, and the full cohort of shlichim who served that year.

What is on the canvas

Jerusalem at the centre, with a thread of light out to every community. Fly to a region, or click a cluster to open it. Loaded with 49 communities to prove the layout holds.

A single year

Memphis, 5768. The Rosh Kollel with his household, the eight shlichim who came that year, and the photographs with the details each one carries.

How to look at it

  1. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom. The three boards sit side by side on one canvas, with notes beside each explaining the thinking. A second page, Not chosen, holds two earlier directions kept for the record.
  2. Press ▶ above a board to interact with it. On the map that turns on the flying: choose a region under “Fly to”, click a numbered cluster to open it, or click any community to load its thirty years into the bottom band.
  3. Export gives you PNG or PDF if you would rather circulate stills than a link.

Where this is going

  1. This site — the public map and timeline, plus a back office to manage communities, years, leaders, shlichim and photographs without touching code.
  2. An upload page where anyone can contribute a photograph, screened automatically before it reaches the archive.
  3. A WhatsApp number that collects photographs by conversation and asks for the details needed to place them.
  4. A campaign dashboard showing where the holes are — which community, which year, still has nothing.
